Inflate to Fill privacy policy

Effective date: 12 November 2026

Inflate to Fill is made by Hyunho Kim. This is the privacy policy for the Inflate to Fill apps for iPhone and Android. It says what the game keeps, what it sends, and who sees it.

한국어 (Korean)

No account, no name, no contact details

You never sign up and never type anything about yourself into the game. We don't ask for your name, email, phone number, contacts or photos. The game never asks for your location.

What stays on your phone

Your best runs, the run you're in the middle of, your daily puzzle streak and today's result, your Adventure stars, which tips you've seen, your settings, the year of birth you gave (see "Children"), and a few plain counts of how you've played are saved in the app's own storage on your phone. We never receive them. Deleting the app deletes them from the phone.

If your phone backs up app data to your Apple or Google account, a copy may be kept there for you. That is between you and Apple or Google. We can't see it.

We don't collect anything ourselves

The game doesn't send your scores, your play or any identifier to us. We run no server for the game, and there is no analytics or crash reporting of our own.

Ads

The game shows ads from Google AdMob. Unless you allow more on an iPhone (below), we ask Google for non-personalised ads only. These are chosen from the app you're using and roughly where you are (about city level), not from what you've done before.

On iPhone: the tracking question

The first time you open the game, once its first screen is showing, your iPhone asks whether the game may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites. The game asks this once, and only if the year of birth you gave means you are 16 or older (see "Children" below). Younger players are never asked and get non-personalised ads only.

  • If you choose Allow, Google's ad software in the game may use your iPhone's advertising identifier and your activity in other apps and websites to choose the ads you see, and to measure them.
  • If you choose Ask App Not to Track, we ask Google for non-personalised ads only, and your iPhone doesn't give the game its advertising identifier.

The game plays the same either way. You can change your answer at any time in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security › Tracking.

On Android

Android has no such question, and the game doesn't ask for anything like it. We ask Google for non-personalised ads only.

What Google's ad software collects

To show ads, count them, limit how often you see the same one and stop fraud, Google's ad software inside the game collects these things and sends them to Google:

  • your IP address, which Google may use to work out roughly where you are;
  • identifiers for your device, or for this app on your device. On iPhone this includes Apple's advertising identifier only if you chose Allow. On Android it can include the phone's advertising ID, an ID shared by one developer's apps on the phone (Android's app set ID) and, where they apply, identifiers tied to accounts signed in on the phone;
  • which ads you see and tap, and how you use the app, such as launches, taps and video views;
  • how the app is running, such as launch time, freezes and battery use, and on iPhone, crash reports.

On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in the phone's settings. Google handles this data under its own policies: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Consent in Europe

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent message before any ad. It asks whether ads may use identifiers on your device. You can change your answer any time in Settings › Privacy choices.

On iPhone this message comes before the tracking question. In these countries an ad is personalised only if you agreed here and also chose Allow.

Reporting an ad

If an ad seems inappropriate or wrong for children, go to Settings › Report an ad. It opens an email to us in your mail app. You see everything in it, and nothing is sent unless you send it.

Purchases

This version of the game has nothing to buy. The game asks the App Store and Google Play nothing about purchases, so no payment or purchase data comes from it to anyone.

Sharing your daily result

After a daily puzzle win, Share hands a short text (the date, stars, taps, time, tries and your streak) to your phone's share sheet. If the share sheet can't open, the text is copied instead. It goes only where you send it.

Emails you send us

If you email us, for support or to report an ad, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to answer you and to deal with the ad, and we don't share them.

Keeping and deleting data

The game sends nothing to us, so we hold no data about you from the game and there is nothing of yours from it for us to delete. What is saved on your phone goes when you delete the app.

We keep an email conversation for 12 months after its last message, then delete it. We delete it sooner if you ask. Copies left in our email provider's backups are gone once that provider's own backup period has passed.

Google's ad software sends its data to Google over an encrypted connection, and Google keeps it under the policies linked above. Google says it makes advertising data in its server logs anonymous by removing part of the IP address after 9 months and cookie information after 18 months.

Children

The game is for a general audience and is not directed at children. It doesn't knowingly collect personal information from children. The game sends nothing to us, and we ask Google to limit ads to its G rating (general audiences).

The first time you open the game, before any ad and before any privacy question, it asks what year you were born. No year is picked for you, and any answer lets you play. The year stays on your phone until you delete the app: it is never sent to us or to anyone else. The game counts your age as this year minus your birth year, minus one more, so it never thinks you are older than you might be.

Each time the game starts, before any ad, it also asks the App Store or Google Play for the age range the store holds for your account, where the store gives one: on iPhone only in places where the law requires it, and on Android where Google Play's age signals cover your account. The game goes by whichever answer is younger: your year, or the youngest age in the store's range. The store's answer is kept in the phone's memory only while the game runs. It is never saved, and never sent to us, to Google's ad software or to anyone else.

  • Under 14: we ask Google to treat the game's ad requests as directed at a child. The iPhone tracking question is never asked, and ads are non-personalised.
  • 14 or 15: we ask Google to treat the ad requests as coming from someone under the age of consent. The iPhone tracking question is never asked, and ads are non-personalised.
  • 16 or older: as described in "Ads" above.

With each ad request the game sends Google only that setting, never the year or the store's answer. The videos you choose to watch work the same at every age.

Children's privacy (United States)

This is our notice under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act. We never ask a child for personal information, the game sends us nothing, and nobody can make personal information public through the game. One thing happens when the age the game goes by is under 13, and this part describes it.

What is collected from a child, and what for

For a player under 13, every ad request is marked as directed at a child. Google says that it then turns off ads based on interests and remarketing for that request, and on Android the phone's advertising ID is not sent. Google's ad software still collects the IP address, an identifier for the device or for the app on it, and how the app is running. These are used only to keep the game's ads working:

  • to show an ad chosen from the game itself, not from what the child has done (contextual advertising);
  • to limit how often the same ad is shown, and to count ads;
  • to detect fraud and keep the ads secure;
  • to keep the ad software running properly.

They are not used to contact a child, to build a profile of a child, or for ads based on behaviour. What makes sure of that: the child-directed mark on every ad request, ads limited to Google's G rating, no tracking question, and the fact that we receive none of it, so we cannot use or pass it on.

Our data retention policy

  • Anything from the game: we receive nothing, so we keep nothing.
  • The year of birth: kept on the phone only, to decide how ads are asked for, until the app is deleted.
  • The store's age answer: not kept at all. It is in the phone's memory only while the game runs.
  • Emails sent to us: kept to answer them and to follow up in the same conversation, for 12 months after the last message, then deleted; sooner if you ask.
  • What Google's ad software collects: kept by Google under its own policies, described in "Keeping and deleting data" above.

What a parent can do

We hold no personal information from a child, unless the child emailed us. Write to us and we will tell you what we hold, delete it, and keep no more. To stop the ad software collecting anything further, delete the app. On Android you can also reset or delete the phone's advertising ID. To ask Google about what its software collected, use Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Why we are allowed to use data

  • Ads that use identifiers on your phone, and personalised ads: your consent. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland you give or refuse it in Google's consent message, and you can take it back any time in Settings › Privacy choices.
  • Emails you send us: our legitimate interest in answering people who write to us. You can object by email.
  • Your progress saved on your phone: to give you the game you asked for.

You never have to give us anything to play, and no law or contract requires it. If you refuse consent for ads, you see less tailored ads or none, and the game plays the same.

Who receives data, and where it goes

  • Google, for ads and the consent message. For players in Europe this is Google Ireland Limited; elsewhere Google's privacy policy covers Google LLC, in the United States, and its affiliates. Google keeps data on servers in the United States and other countries. The advertising partners named in the consent message can receive it too.
  • Our email provider, Google LLC (Gmail), in the United States, for emails you send us. We are in South Korea, so your email comes there.

When data leaves the EEA, the UK or Switzerland: Google says it uses the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, to which it is certified, and standard contractual clauses where those are needed (https://policies.google.com/privacy/frameworks). You can read these at the links, or ask us for a copy.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of the emails we hold from you, ask us to correct or delete them, to stop or limit our use of them, or to send them to you in a form another service can read. You can object to our use of them, and take back any consent. Write to monomadstudio@gmail.com from the address you used before. That is how we know the emails are yours, and we ask for nothing else. We answer within one month, free of charge.

We can't see or delete what Google's ad software collected: we never receive it. To control it, use Settings › Privacy choices (in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), the tracking switch in your iPhone's Settings, or the advertising ID settings on your Android phone. You can also ask Google under its privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

If you think we have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to the data protection authority where you live. The authorities in the EEA are listed at https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en. If you live in Korea, the Korean version of this policy says how to use your rights there.

We make no decisions about you by automated means. Google's software chooses which ad to show.
